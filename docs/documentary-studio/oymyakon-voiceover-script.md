# Oymyakon: The Place Where Standing Still Can Kill You

5 minute voiceover script (about 780 words). Figures come from the source script and have not been independently verified, so check them before publishing.

## 0:00 to 0:25 | Cold open
Visual: frozen wolf in the snow, wind howling.

NARRATOR: This wolf did not die in a fight. It stopped moving. That is all it took. In this village, standing still can kill you. It is minus 60 degrees Celsius, and the cold is not looking for a fight. It is waiting for a single mistake. Stay with me, because by the end you'll understand why one of the things we did here was almost a mistake we couldn't undo.

## 0:25 to 0:55 | The journey
Visual: map, plane, vehicle on an icy road.

NARRATOR: To reach this place, we flew from Moscow to Yakutsk. Seven hours. Then came the real test. Eighteen hours. Nine hundred kilometers. Frozen roads, no mercy. At 2 a.m., we arrived in Oymyakon. The thermometer read minus 60.5. We opened the door. And it felt like stepping onto another planet.

## 0:55 to 1:35 | The first minutes
Visual: frost forming on eyelashes, host's nose turning white.

NARRATOR: The hairs inside our noses froze instantly. Every breath stung. Within seconds, ice crystals grew on our eyelashes. Around 800 people live here. But right now, the village looks abandoned. Why? Because anyone outside has a reason... and a deadline.
HOST: Is this frostbite?
LOCAL GUIDE: Yes.
NARRATOR: Fifteen minutes. That's all it took.

## 1:35 to 2:05 | The science of damage
Visual: animated blood vessels constricting.

NARRATOR: Here's what happens inside your body. The cold squeezes your blood vessels shut. Water inside your cells freezes. Sharp ice crystals tear the tissue apart. And if the skin turns black? The tissue may already be dying. After that, even ten minutes outside became too dangerous.

## 2:05 to 2:40 | Survival gear
Visual: layers going on, one by one.

NARRATOR: So how do people survive out here? Twenty layers. Almost 14 kilograms of clothing. Gloves, boots, and coats made from reindeer fur. Stepping out of a 30 degree home meant a drop of almost 100 degrees. And still, the cold found a way in. Then we heard something strange. When people breathed out, it sounded like dry grass rustling behind them. That sound? Warm breath, turning to ice.

## 2:40 to 3:15 | The house that fights back
Visual: wooden house, pipes, frost-covered entrance room.

NARRATOR: Everything here depends on one thing: keeping the heat in. The ground is permafrost, frozen for hundreds of meters down. So houses are built on thick wooden beams, with layers of insulation, triple-glazed windows, and walls packed with basalt wool and foam. But the heat itself comes from one place. A single coal boiler. It cannot stop. Not even briefly. If it does, pipes freeze. Homes go cold. And lives are put at risk. Their refrigerator is the unheated entrance room, covered in frost.
RESIDENT: This is our refrigerator.

## 3:15 to 3:50 | The barn and the horses
Visual: cows in the dark with cloth around udders, Yakutian horses in deep snow.

NARRATOR: Before sunrise, Yevdokia is already in the barn. The cows' udders are wrapped in cloth, to stop them from freezing. There are no pipes. So the animals walk to the river, and their coats ice over within minutes. Then I felt it. The left side of my face went numb.
LOCAL GUIDE: It is frostbite. Cover it immediately.
NARRATOR: But some animals don't need shelter at all. The Yakutian horse survives below minus 70, outdoors, with no heated barn.

## 3:50 to 4:30 | The hunt
Visual: frozen river, fishing net, Ivan, a hidden trap.

NARRATOR: Now we follow Ivan, across a frozen river. The ice is thin in places. One wrong step, and you're in the water. And wet clothes, at this temperature, freeze almost instantly. He pulls a fish from the ice. It stays alive for only a few seconds. Then the cold takes over. He eats it frozen. But first, he gives the fire a small offering. Later, we check a trap. Inside is a hare. It wasn't killed by the trap. It was frozen in place.

## 4:30 to 4:55 | The real lesson
Visual: children walking to school, faces covered.

NARRATOR: Even ordinary life runs on strict rules. A bath takes four or five hours. Cars need three or four hours of heating before they'll start. Schools close below about minus 55. Today? Minus 51. So the children walk, faces covered. Remember the wolf?
IVAN: If an animal stops moving in this cold, it can die.
NARRATOR: The same rule applies to people.

## 4:55 to 5:20 | Close
Visual: chimney smoke, warm windows, snow at night, fade to black.

NARRATOR: But Oymyakon is not a story about dying. It's a story about living. These people don't defeat the winter. They prepare for it. They respect it. A warm house. A working boiler. Dry clothes. Food and water. And inside those walls, children grow up, families celebrate, and ordinary life goes on. Not by conquering the cold... but by learning to live beside it.

On-screen text: Oymyakon, Sakha Republic, Russia. One of the coldest inhabited places on Earth.
