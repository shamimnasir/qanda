## 5. Hooks (viral-hook-generator skill, English)
**Topic:** Why Japanese people live longer | **Audience:** curious viewers 18 to 40, health and travel interest | **Platform:** YouTube Shorts / Reels

1. "153 Japanese people were over 100 in 1963. Today, 107,677."
   Format: Result First (93) · Why: two hard numbers make the jump impossible to ignore.
   On-screen text: "153 → 107,677"
2. "Japan once had the world's worst stroke rate, by one estimate. Then it fixed it."
   Format: Contrarian Claim (98) · Why: breaks the "Japanese were always healthy" belief.
   On-screen text: "Japan wasn't always healthy"
3. "The island famous for long life fell to 43rd for men."
   Format: Curiosity Gap (100) · Why: an open loop the viewer needs closed.
   On-screen text: "Okinawa's long-life twist"
4. "Stop eating at 80 percent full. Okinawa has a saying for it."
   Format: Hidden Reveal (92) · Why: an insider phrase with a simple rule.
   On-screen text: "Hara hachi bu: 80% full"
5. "No reason to get up in the morning? One study found 1.5 times the risk."
   Format: Direct Question + Result First · Why: personal, specific, a little unsettling.
   On-screen text: "Ikigai: 1.5x"
6. "Forget superfoods. Japan's biggest health win started with less salt."
   Format: Negative Command · Why: rejects a cliché, names a concrete swap.
   On-screen text: "It started with salt"
7. "In 2010, Tokyo's 'oldest man' had been dead for about thirty years."
   Format: In Medias Res · Why: a shocking true scene, then a turn.
   On-screen text: "Tokyo, 2010"
8. "Friends may matter as much as quitting smoking, says a 148-study review."
   Format: Contrarian Claim · Why: unexpected comparison with a named source size.
   On-screen text: "Friends vs smoking"
9. "If you think it's genes, watch what happened to Okinawa."
   Format: Identity Call / Mistake Warning · Why: challenges a belief and promises proof.
   On-screen text: "Not just genes"
10. "Japan gave everyone health insurance in 1961. Here's what came after."
    Format: Timeframe / Story · Why: a date anchors a story arc.
    On-screen text: "1961 changed Japan"

**Top pick:** hook 1, because the first frame can show the two numbers.
**Test note:** A/B hook 1 against hook 3.
Note on hook 8: Holt-Lunstad compared the size of the effect to smoking. "As much as" is the authors' comparison, not a measured equality: keep "may".

## 7. Footage honesty flags
- Stock people are illustrative. Several are not Japanese (the doctor, the gardener, the painter, the card players, the couples in nature and by the sea, the friends on the sofa). They sit under general lines (purpose, social ties, "anyone can start today"), not under lines about specific Japanese people. Check you are comfortable with them.
- Swapped after the frame check: a staged "scientist" looking at the camera under the Blue Zones line (now a back view), a stranger at a market under "Okinawan elders" (now food only), a Western man under "behind them" (now a Tokyo street), a cemetery-like path, and a shot too dark to read.
- S01b / S61: older visitors at a Japanese temple, including a woman in a wheelchair. They appear under "Japan counted its oldest people" and "remember those 107,677 people". Nothing says she is a centenarian, but if you want zero risk, swap the footage.
- E01: the end card uses a real Itsukushima torii (Hiroshima), labelled only "JAPAN". It is not Okinawa.
- S07 (stormy sea) and S46 (dark clouds) are mood shots, location unknown.
