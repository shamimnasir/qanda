| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "Most toxic town in America" | Common label. NPR and OHS rank it first among Superfund sites, but the ranking is not confirmed in an EPA source |
| 14,000 mine shafts | OHS says 1,400 shafts. 14,000 looks like the number of mine workers |
| 86% of buildings undermined | The cited Corps report could not be opened, and NPR reports about one-third |
| 50% of bullets in both World Wars | OHS supports World War I metal only |
| Peak population of 5,000, or 25,000 | OHS gives 14,252. The 25,000 is one resident's memory in NPR |
| 63% of children, 62.5% of children | No clear source, year or method |
| Reading scores | NPR reports them, but the film should not imply a cause it cannot prove |

## Math and consistency checks
- 14,252 to 20 is a loss of 14,232 people, 99.9%. Not stated in the script.
- February to May 10, 2008: about 3 months.
- 1967 to 2009: 42 years. "Decades earlier" holds for mining from 1913 to 1967.

- Picher (locals say PITCH-er: confirm before voicing), Quapaw (KWAH-paw), Keheley, Crawfish, Tar Creek, Superfund, EF4 (spelled E F four), EPA (spelled E P A).
